## Section 3: Diagnostics overview

**[H2]** Our diagnostics

**Intro:**
Each test looks at one part of the endometrial picture. None of them is a stand-alone answer, and no single test is right for every patient. The treating clinician decides which test, if any, fits the case.

We show the evidence status of every test so that you can make that decision with the full picture.

**Card 1 (lead card, wider or first)**
**[H3]** Endometrial Infection Test (EIT)
Looks for chronic endometritis, a low-grade inflammation of the uterine lining that often causes no symptoms.
**Evidence chip (green):** Guideline: assessment "can be considered" in recurrent implantation failure (ESHRE 2023)
**[Link]** About the EIT → `/diagnostics/endometrial-infection-test/`

**Card 2**
**[H3]** ERFA, The Soil Test
Endometrial Receptivity and Function Assay. Our in-house assay profiles markers of endometrial receptivity and function. Indian patent application published (No. 202431001616 A).
**Evidence chip (neutral):** Evidence: emerging. Not for routine use; for selected cases on clinician advice.
**[Link]** About ERFA → `/diagnostics/erfa-endometrial-receptivity-test/`

Profiles immune markers in the endometrium, including uterine NK cells and cytokines. {{CONFIRM_ENDO_IMMUNE_MARKERS}}

## Section 4: How the sample journey works

**[H2]** From your clinic to a report in 7 to 10 days

**Intro:** One process for every test. Your patient stays with you throughout; we handle the lab work and report back to you.

**Step 1**
**[H3]** The clinician orders
The treating doctor decides a test is appropriate, explains it to the patient, takes written consent and completes our requisition form.

**Step 2**
**[H3]** The sample is collected
The clinician takes an endometrial biopsy at the timing the test needs, at their own clinic or at a collection partner. It is packed in our kit and labelled. {{CONFIRM_KIT_PROVIDED}}

**Step 3**
**[H3]** The sample travels to our lab
The sample is sent to our lab in Mohali {{TRANSPORT_CONDITIONS e.g. at room temperature in fixative / chilled}}, with the requisition form and chain-of-custody record.

**Step 4**
**[H3]** You receive the report
The report is sent to the ordering clinician within 7 to 10 days of the sample reaching us. Our scientific team is available to discuss the findings.

**Microcopy (small, below steps):** Transit delays or samples that need repeat processing can extend turnaround. We will tell you if that happens. Samples that do not meet our acceptance criteria may need to be recollected. {{CONFIRM}}

## Section 9: FAQ (short, homepage)

**[H2]** Common questions

**[H3]** Is EmbryoGenesis an IVF centre?
No. We are a diagnostics lab and academy. We do not offer IVF. We test samples ordered by IVF clinicians and gynaecologists, and we report back to them.

**[H3]** Can I book a test myself?
Our tests are ordered by a doctor. If you think a test may be relevant, talk to your fertility specialist. They can order it and collect the sample. {{CONFIRM_DIRECT_PATIENT_POLICY}}

**[H3]** How long do results take?
Usually 7 to 10 days from the day the sample reaches our lab. The report goes to the doctor who ordered the test.

**[H3]** Where can samples be collected?
At the ordering clinic, or at one of our collection partners in Navi Mumbai, Udaipur and Jalandhar. Our main lab is in Mohali, Punjab.

**[H3]** Why don't you show prices?
Prices depend on the clinic arrangement. Clinics can request our partner rate card. Patients should ask the clinic that orders the test.

**[H3]** Will these tests improve my chance of pregnancy?
No test can promise that. A test can help your doctor find or rule out a specific problem. What happens next is a clinical decision made with your doctor.
