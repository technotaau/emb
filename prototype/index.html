    Evidence status

    What the guidelines say

    ESHRE's 2023 good-practice recommendations on recurrent implantation failure state that testing for chronic endometritis can be considered, while routine endometrial receptivity testing and immune profiling are not recommended for routine use pending better evidence. We present each test with that status so you can decide case by case.

    References: ESHRE RIF good practice recommendations (2023); ESHRE RPL guideline (2022). Full list on each test page.

    Diagnostics · Endometrial Infection Test

    Endometrial Infection Test (chronic endometritis)

    A biopsy-based test that looks for persistent, usually symptom-free inflammation of the uterine lining. Chronic endometritis is treatable, and ESHRE says testing can be considered after repeated implantation failure.

    Sample: endometrial biopsyMethod: {{METHOD}}Report: 7–10 days
    Quick answer

    What it is: a lab test for chronic endometritis.
    Who orders it: your treating doctor.
    Who it may help: people with repeated IVF failure, recurrent pregnancy loss or unexplained infertility.
    What it is not: a treatment or a guarantee of pregnancy.

    For clinicians

    IndicationsRIF after transfer of good-quality embryos; RPL; unexplained infertility; suspected CE on hysteroscopy
    SampleEndometrial biopsy, pipelle, {{VOLUME}}
    Timing{{TIMING}} · avoid within 2 weeks of antibiotics
    Method{{METHOD e.g. CD138 immunohistochemistry ± culture/PCR}}
    ReportPlasma cell count per {{UNIT}}, interpretation, suggested follow-up per literature
    Ordering & transport

    For patients

    Chronic endometritis rarely causes symptoms. Your doctor may suggest this test if embryos of good quality have not implanted, or if you have had more than one miscarriage. The biopsy takes a few minutes in the clinic. If the test is positive, treatment is usually a course of antibiotics followed by a repeat test.

    This test can tell you
    • whether plasma cells (a marker of chronic inflammation) are present in the lining
    • whether treatment has cleared them (on repeat)
    This test cannot tell you
    • whether a pregnancy will happen
    • whether your embryos are healthy
    • anything on its own; it is read with your full history
    Evidence status

    ESHRE's 2023 RIF recommendations state that testing for chronic endometritis can be considered. Observational studies associate treated CE with improved implantation in some cohorts; randomised evidence is limited. We state this plainly so clinicians and patients can weigh it.

    1. ESHRE Working Group on RIF, Hum Reprod Open 2023. 2. Cicinelli E et al., Hum Reprod 2015. 3. Kitaya K et al., Am J Reprod Immunol 2017. {{VERIFY}}

    Frequently asked

    Does the biopsy hurt?

    Most people describe cramping for a few minutes. Your clinic will advise on pain relief.

    Can the same biopsy be used for ERFA and the Endo-Immune Test?

    Yes. One sample can be split across the three panels when the clinician orders them together.

    How do I get this test?

    Ask your doctor. Tests are ordered by clinicians and processed through our collection centres.

    What does it cost?

    Your clinic or collection centre will quote the price. We do not publish prices online.
